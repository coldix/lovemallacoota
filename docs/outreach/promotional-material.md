# Promotional material

Copy for announcing Love Mallacoota, ready to paste. Written to be said out
loud by one person to a town of a thousand, not shouted by a brand.

Everything here is true as at 30 August 2026. Check the numbers before reusing
it later — an out-of-date claim on a site whose whole pitch is accuracy is
worse than no claim.

---

## The one-liner

> Love Mallacoota — the town's directory, weekly edition and archive. Free to
> read, free to be listed in.

## The short version, for a Facebook post

> **Something to replace the Mouth.**
>
> Since the Mallacoota Mouth closed there has been nowhere that just tells you
> what is on, who is open and what happened. So I have built one.
>
> lovemallacoota.au has a directory of local businesses and groups, the
> community calendar, the week's weather and tides, and from this week a weekly
> edition — notices, club news, local history, classifieds and family notices.
> There is a printable copy each week if you would rather have it on paper.
>
> It is free to read and free to be listed in. Nothing is behind a login.
>
> Two things I need: tell me what is wrong or missing, and tell me what you want
> in it. It is meant to be the town's, not mine.
>
> #MallacootaNow

## The longer version, for a group or a noticeboard

> **A weekly for Mallacoota, and an archive of the old one**
>
> When *The Mallacoota Mouth* stopped, the town lost the one place that
> collected what was happening. Facebook does some of it, but nothing there is
> findable a month later, and not everyone is on it.
>
> lovemallacoota.au now carries:
>
> - **A weekly edition** — notices, club and school news, local history,
>   classifieds, family notices, what is on, the forecast, the tides and the
>   moon. Published Sunday afternoon, with a printable copy.
> - **A directory** of places to eat, stay and visit, and of local groups and
>   services. Being listed is free and always will be. Each listing shows when
>   it was last checked, so you can judge how much to trust it.
> - **Local of the Week** — one person's story, every week. The first is Frank
>   Stokes, who dived the Snowy and the Barwon for the PMG in 1968.
> - **An archive** of *The Mallacoota Mouth* — 37 issues catalogued so far, with
>   the contents of each, so a name or a club can be found.
>
> It is run by one person, it costs money to keep going, and there is no
> advertising you have to look past to read it. If it is useful, you can chip in
> or become a supporter; if you have a business, an ad is $35 a month and the
> listing stays free either way.
>
> What I want most is corrections. Tell me what is wrong.

## For a business, in person or by email

> I run lovemallacoota.au — the local directory and the new weekly edition.
>
> You are already listed, free, and that will not change. Have a look and tell
> me if anything is wrong: hours, phone number, whether you are open at all this
> season. It takes me a minute to fix and it saves your customers ringing a
> number that does not answer.
>
> If you want more than a listing, an advertisement is $35 a month and runs in
> the weekly edition, in print and on the site. No contract; stop whenever you
> like.

## For a club or community group

> lovemallacoota.au has a section for local groups, and a weekly edition that
> will print your notices, results and meeting times for nothing.
>
> If you nominate someone, they can publish straight to it — no going through
> me, no waiting, no charge. Meeting changes, working bees, results, calls for
> members.
>
> It is free because a town works better when everybody knows what everybody
> else is doing.

## What not to claim

- **Do not say the archive is available to read.** It is catalogued, not
  published, and the difference is a copyright permission the College has not
  been asked for yet.
- **Do not call contributions donations without qualification.** They are not
  tax deductible, and the site says so.
- **Do not promise a newsletter by email.** There is not one yet.
- **Do not describe the tide times as tide tables.** They are modelled sea
  level, indicative, and not for navigation.
