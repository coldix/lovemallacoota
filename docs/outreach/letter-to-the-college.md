# Letter to Mallacoota P-12 College

About *The Mallacoota Mouth* archive. Two requests, and a fallback if the answer
to both is no.

**Send to:** the Principal, Mallacoota P-12 College
**From:** Colin Dixon, Love Mallacoota (lovemallacoota.au)
**Attach or link:** https://lovemallacoota.au/archive.html

---

Dear [Principal's name],

I am writing about *The Mallacoota Mouth*, and about something I have built that
I would rather you saw early than discovered later.

Since the Mouth closed, I have been putting together a free community
information service for the town at lovemallacoota.au — a directory of local
businesses and groups, a community calendar, and since this month a weekly
edition carrying notices, club and school news, local history and the things the
Mouth used to carry. It is free to read, free to be listed in, and there is no
paywall planned, ever.

Part of that is an archive. I have catalogued 37 issues of the Mouth from 2020
to 2023 — issue number, date, editor, page count, cover, and the contents of
each edition, so that someone looking for a name, an event or a club can find
which issue it appeared in. You can see it at
https://lovemallacoota.au/archive.html

**I have not published a single page of any issue, and I will not without your
agreement.** The catalogue lists what exists; the PDFs stay offline. Every entry
is marked as requiring a decision on rights, because the Mouth was produced by
the College with community volunteers and the copyright is not mine to give
away.

So, two questions.

**First: would the College give permission for the issues to be published
online, free to read?** They would sit alongside the catalogue, credited to the
College, in a form that can be searched — which is the difference between a
box of paper in a cupboard and a local history anyone can use. Twenty years from
now, someone tracing a family or a club will find them because they are
searchable. If the College would prefer conditions — a delay before recent
issues appear, particular issues withheld, a specific credit line, the right to
ask for something to be taken down — I will take them as given and write them
into the site's published policy.

**Second, if the answer to that is no: do you have any objection to the
catalogue itself?** The catalogue holds no reproduced content, only a
description of each issue and what was in it. I think it is useful on its own,
and it is already public. If the College would rather it were not, tell me and I
will take it down without argument.

There is a third thing I would value more than either.

**Would the College consider contributing to the weekly edition?** There is a
section for it — school news, the principal's report, sport, the things that
used to run under "Out and About at MP-12". Anyone the College nominates can
publish straight to the site without going through me, and nothing is edited or
charged for. If a student wanted to write, that is a byline in a real
publication that will still exist when they are older.

I am one person doing this on my own time, and I would rather build it with the
College than around it. I am happy to come in and show anyone how it works, and
equally happy to be told to slow down.

Yours sincerely,

Colin Dixon
Love Mallacoota · lovemallacoota.au
coota@lovemallacoota.au

## Notes before sending

- Put the Principal's name in. A letter addressed to a title reads as a
  circular.
- The two questions are deliberately separable. It is much easier for a school
  to say "no to publishing, no objection to the catalogue" than to weigh one
  answer covering both — and that answer alone lets the archive stand.
- Do not attach the PDFs. Offering the material back to the people who made it
  looks like a fait accompli.
- If there is no reply in three weeks, ring rather than email again. A school is
  not ignoring you; a school is busy.
- Whatever comes back, record it in `docs/ARCHIVE.md` with the date and who said
  it. A verbal yes from a principal who later moves on is worth nothing unless
  it is written down.
